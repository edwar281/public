Lending Club Credit Study

Did the Rate Cover the Risk?

Credit losses, realized investor returns and a profit-based approval policy across 2.26 million Lending Club loans

Will Edwards, PhDOctober 2026Code on GitHub

Executive summary

What we found

    About this analysis

    Lending Club was the largest US marketplace lender of the 2010s. It graded each borrower from A to G, set an interest rate for each of 35 sub-grades, and sold the loans to investors. Its public loan file covers every loan issued from 2007 through 2018 ( loans, ), with each loan's outcome as of March 2019, plus rejected applications.

    A lender's pricing is a bet that each grade's interest rate will more than cover its losses. This study checks that bet loan by loan: how losses built up by vintage, what investors actually earned in each grade once defaults and recoveries came in, and whether a model built only on information available at application could have picked out the loans that lost money.

    1How much was lent, and how fast loans went bad

    2What investors actually earned

    The matured book is every 36-month loan issued from 2010 through 2015 ( loans, ), all of which had reached their final status by the data cutoff. For each loan, monthly cash flows are rebuilt from its installment, the total it paid, and when it stopped paying. Recoveries arrive six months after charge-off, and the investor pays Lending Club's 1% servicing fee. The realized return is what investors actually earned per year, like an interest rate after losses.

    3Could a model have found the money-losing loans?

    How it was tested. The model learned from loans made in 2013 and 2014, then was asked to score every 2015 loan it had never seen, using only what Lending Club knew on the day each application came in. Because every 2015 loan has since been paid off or charged off, its predictions can be checked against what actually happened, in dollars.

    Technical detail: model accuracy and the inputs that drive it

    The model is LightGBM, trained on 36-month loans issued January 2013 to September 2014 ( loans), tuned on the fourth quarter of 2014, and scored once on all 36-month loans issued in 2015 ( loans, ). Inputs are credit bureau attributes, income, debt-to-income, employment, loan purpose and state. It never sees payment history or later credit pulls.

    4Data and methods

    Data and definitions

    Lending Club accepted and rejected loan files, 2007–2018Q4, as published on Kaggle (wordsforthewise/lending-club), with loan status as of March 2019. A loan is bad if it was charged off, in default, or 31–120 days late at the cutoff. The file gives the last payment month but not the charge-off date, so charge-off is placed five months after the last payment, matching Lending Club's 150-day charge-off rule. Vintage curves count a loan at month m only if it had been on the books for at least m months by the cutoff.

    Returns

    Each loan's payments are spread as regular installments up to its last payment month, scaled to the total actually received, with any excess treated as a prepayment in the final month. Recoveries net of collection fees arrive six months after charge-off. Pool IRRs solve for the monthly rate that equates pooled cash flows with dollars lent and are annualized. Lifetime return in Section 3 is total cash received minus dollars lent, divided by dollars lent, without discounting.

    Modeling and policy

    Validation is out-of-time: the model never sees 2015 loans while training or tuning. Bureau fields that Lending Club only began reporting in late 2015 are excluded. The approval policy declines the riskiest k% of 2015 loans under each ranking and sums the realized profit of the rest. Because Lending Club's sub-grade has only 35 values, ties within the marginal sub-grade are broken at random. SHAP values are LightGBM's built-in tree contributions.

    Data: Lending Club loan data 2007–2018, via Kaggle. Lending Club is not affiliated with this work.
    Limitations: one lender's approved population (the model cannot learn about rejected applicants); returns are reconstructed from summary payment fields, not monthly payment records; profit ignores funding costs and the time value of money; results are associations.
