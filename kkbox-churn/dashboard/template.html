Portfolio case study · Subscription churn

Who leaves, and when: churn across 6.8 million subscribers

Will Edwards, PhDAnalytics & Data Science leader · 20+ years in telecom and financial servicesCode on GitHub

A subscription business loses customers at renewal. Using public data from KKBox, a large Asian music-streaming service with carrier-style billing, I measured when subscribers leave, what predicts it, and how well next month's churners can be found ahead of time.

392M daily usage rows 23M billing transactions 6.8M member profiles 564K new paid subscribers tracked over time 1.96M labelled renewal decisions
Headline result · out-of-time test, March 2017
61% of churners

were among the 10% of subscribers the model ranked riskiest, scored before the month began. That is 6.1× better than random targeting, so a retention team can reach most at-risk customers while contacting a small fraction of the base.

Chapter 1 · Survival analysis

When do new subscribers leave?

I followed every genuinely new paying subscriber who joined between March 2015 and December 2016 until they churned or the data ended. Customers still active at the end are counted as censored, not as retained, so later sign-ups don't flatter the numbers.

Chapter 2 · Predictive model

Can we find next month's churners in advance?

I trained a gradient-boosted model on 993K subscribers whose memberships expired in February 2017, using only information available before February began. I then tested it, untouched, on 971K subscribers expiring in March. This is how the model would actually be used: trained on last month, scored on next month.

Methods & data

How this was built

Data and definitions

  • Source: WSDM-KKBox Churn Prediction Challenge (Kaggle), released by KKBox for research. Member IDs are anonymized.
  • Churn follows the company's own rule: no new paid subscription within 30 days of the membership expiring. Cancelling pulls the expiry date forward.
  • New-subscriber cohort: registered in 2015 or later, first paid transaction between Mar 2015 and Dec 2016. Requiring two months of history before the first payment avoids mistaking returning members for new ones (left truncation).

Modelling choices

  • Point-in-time features: every input is dated before the month in which the label is decided. That rules out leakage from a renewal that has already happened.
  • Out-of-time test on the following month, rather than a random split, which would overstate accuracy.
  • LightGBM, early-stopped on a 15% in-time holdout. Calibration repaired by re-estimating the intercept and slope on a 5% labelled slice of the new month, then evaluated on the other 95%.
  • Survival: Kaplan–Meier with Greenwood bands; Cox proportional hazards on a 200K sample, stratified by sign-up quarter; non-proportionality checked with month-by-month hazards.

Engineering at scale

  • A 30 GB CSV of daily listening logs, streamed into compressed Parquet (4.4 GB) with integer keys, on a 2-core, 8 GB machine.
  • DuckDB for all joins and window functions; features for about 2M labelled subscribers built in a few minutes.
  • Fully reproducible pipeline: download, then spells, survival, features and model as five scripts.
