Subscription Churn at Scale

Survival analysis and one-month-ahead churn prediction for 6.8 million subscribers

Will Edwards, PhDOctober 2026Code on GitHub

A subscription business loses customers at renewal. Using public data from KKBox, a large Asian music-streaming service with carrier-style billing, this analysis examines predictors of churn and how well we can predict next month's churners.

The data cover 2015 to 2017: 392 million daily usage records, 23 million billing transactions and 6.8 million member profiles. Two questions drive the work. When in the customer lifecycle do new subscribers leave, and what changes that timing? And using only what is known before a month begins, how many of that month's churners can be identified?

Summary

    1When do new subscribers churn?

    The cohort is every new paying subscriber who joined between March 2015 and December 2016 (564K people), followed until they churned or the data ended. Subscribers still active at the end are treated as censored rather than retained, so recent sign-ups do not inflate retention.

    2Predicting next month's churners

    A gradient-boosted model (LightGBM) was trained on 993K subscribers whose memberships expired in February 2017, using only information dated before February 1. It was then scored, without retraining, on 971K subscribers expiring in March. This mirrors production use: fit on last month, score next month.

    3Data and methods

    Data and definitions

    The source is the WSDM-KKBox Churn Prediction Challenge on Kaggle, released by KKBox for research with anonymized member IDs. Churn follows the company's own rule: no new paid subscription within 30 days of the membership expiring, with cancellations moving the expiry date forward. The survival cohort includes members registered in 2015 or later whose first paid transaction falls between March 2015 and December 2016. Requiring two months of prior history avoids mistaking a returning member for a new one.

    Modeling

    All model inputs are dated before the month in which the label is decided, which rules out leakage from renewals that have already happened. Validation is out-of-time on the following month rather than a random split, which would overstate accuracy. The model was early-stopped on a 15% in-time holdout. Calibration was corrected by re-estimating intercept and slope on a 5% labelled sample of the new month and evaluated on the remaining 95%. Survival estimates use Kaplan–Meier with Greenwood intervals and a Cox model on a 200K sample stratified by sign-up quarter, with proportionality checked month by month.

    Data: WSDM-KKBox's Churn Prediction Challenge, Kaggle (KKBox, 2017). KKBox is not affiliated with this work.
    Limitations: one service, 2015–2017; the model is evaluated on a single out-of-time month; effects are associations, not randomized treatment effects (auto-renew customers may differ in commitment before they enroll).
